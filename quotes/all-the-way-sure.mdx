My daughter had just turned 4, and as we drove the winding streets of Colorado Springs she asked if this was the way to the store.

"It is," I said.

"Are you sure?" 

"Pretty sure," I replied (not because I wasn't, but because it reminds me of a dialogue from one of my favorite stories).

_"Why aren't you all the way sure?"_

She asked this with such genuine curiosity, and it opened the door to a fun philosophical conversation.
 
This was my first experience of such an early verbal expression of the desire for certainty. While the conversation touched only on trivial matters, I find the idea of *certainty* thoroughly distasteful. It kills curiosity and stunts intellectual growth.

And it gets worse as one grows older.

For a thorough exposition of how the idea of certainty was regarded among the major figures of the scientific revolution, see Robert Pasneau's _After Certainty_.
